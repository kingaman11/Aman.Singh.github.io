Parking Intelligence Command Center

By Aman Singh & Harkeerat Sidhu

Problem
Make congestion, capacity and overflow information easier to interpret.
Contribution
Co-developed a Streamlit decision-support dashboard with Harkeerat Sidhu.
Outcome
An interactive capstone prototype with executive and deep-dive views, configurable filters, and PDF reporting.

The project explores utilization-based lot recommendations and nearby alternatives, with validation and isolated error handling to keep the dashboard usable.

  • Lot recommendations: ranks parking options for a selected hour using mismatch, underuse and utilization signals.
  • Nearby alternatives: suggests other lots by combining distance with utilization and recommendation signals (GIS-assisted).
  • Reliability: column validation, weekday/weekend handling, tab-level error isolation, and cleaner labels and layout.
  • Transparency: methodology and findings sections are described in the project notes.
  • Scope as documented by the team; the public demo's current views may differ.

15 threshold alerts became 1 notification (93% fewer pages), driven by filtering and grouping — not a measured false-positive rate. Persistence adds a 15-minute detection delay on the first incident.

Data. 96 synthetic 15-minute samples of authorization rate and transaction volume, generated from a fixed seed per scenario. Incident windows are labels used only to score results; the alert logic never reads them.

Threshold alerting. One alert per sample below the threshold.

Simulated AI-assisted triage. Rule-based stand-in for a context layer: ignore breaches below the minimum volume; wait until a breach persists for N consecutive samples (using only past and current samples); group confirmed breaches within one quiet sample into one open notification; write a short explanation from the observed values.

Scoring. Incidents count as detected if any alert or notification lands inside the window. Noise means an alert or notification that touches no incident window. Grouped follow-ons are not counted as noise.

Limits. Educational sketch, not a production recommendation or a real model. Runs entirely in your browser with no network calls.

About

I'm a product and operations professional based in the San Francisco Bay Area. My work sits where payments, data and AI meet — the places where small signals can carry real financial weight.

I've monitored payment performance at Roku, built forecasts and automation at Visa, created dashboards at Chevron and led go-to-market research at Effective AI. Along the way I've supported graduate students in regression and analytics at Georgia Tech.

My focus is expanding toward AI-enabled operations: internal tools, workflow automation, and clearer visibility into delivery health. I'm interested in connecting the operating cadence of a team with the agents and applications that support it.

What ties it together: making complex systems easier to understand, and more reliable to run.
